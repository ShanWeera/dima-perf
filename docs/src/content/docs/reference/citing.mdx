If you use DiMA, please cite **the paper** that describes the method [@tharanga2025] and **the
version of the software** you used. Citing the version lets readers reproduce your results
exactly; citing the paper credits the method.

## The citation

`dima cite` prints both, in the style of the life-science literature:

```sh
dima cite                    # plain text
dima cite --format bibtex    # for LaTeX and most reference managers
dima cite --format ris       # for EndNote, Zotero, Mendeley
dima cite --format cff       # Citation File Format
```

The software's BibTeX key names its version, with underscores for the dots: `dima_rs_1_1_1` for
version 1.1.1, so entries for two versions never clash in one bibliography. Inside a results
file, `run.references` lists the software under the fixed key `dima_software`, with the version
that wrote the file in its text; `dima cite --from` turns it back into the entry of that version.

The repository's `CITATION.cff` holds the same information (GitHub shows it as "Cite this
repository"), with the paper as the preferred citation and the references of every method DiMA
can use.

### The Cite: line

When standard error is a terminal, `dima analyze` ends with a reminder after its
[summary line](/reference/glossary/#summary-line):

```text
Cite: Tharanga et al. 2025, doi:10.1093/bib/bbae607 (`dima cite --from mers.json` lists this run's references)
```

It names the results file only when the results were written to a regular file, quoted for the
shell when the name needs it; otherwise (results on standard output) it says that `dima cite`
prints the references. It is not printed when standard error is redirected to a file or a pipe,
and `-q` hides it.

## The methods of a run

A DiMA analysis can use several methods, each with its own sources: the entropy estimator, the
sample coverage, the motif definitions, alphabet detection, nucleotide ambiguity codes and, when
requested, similarity-weighted entropy. Every results file lists the references of the methods
that run actually used, in `run.references`. `dima cite --from` turns them into a methods
paragraph and a numbered reference list, ready to adapt for a manuscript:

```sh
dima cite --from results.json            # methods paragraph and references, plain text
dima cite --from results.json --format bibtex   # the references only, as BibTeX
dima cite --from results.json --from conserved.json   # with the conserved sequences
```

- **Text only has the paragraph.** With `--format bibtex`, `ris` or `cff`, `dima cite --from`
  prints the run's reference list in that format, without the methods paragraph: those formats
  hold references, not prose.
- **Standard input.** `--from -` reads the file from standard input, for example
  `cat results.json | dima cite --from -`. Results compressed with gzip or Zstandard are read
  as they are, from a file or from standard input.
- **One run.** `--from` takes at most one results file and one conserved-sequences file, and both
  must describe the same run: give the conserved sequences computed from those results
  (`--hcs-output` of the same `dima analyze`, or `dima hcs -i results.json`). Files of different
  runs (another input, k or support threshold) are refused, because the paragraph would mix them.

Conserved sequences are computed separately (`--hcs-output` or `dima hcs`), so their method and
references are in their own file; give it as a second `--from` to add a sentence on them and
their sources (the Clopper–Pearson bounds, the joining rule) to the text and the list.

For the MERS-CoV spike sample with the defaults (`dima analyze -i samples/mers_spike_aa.fasta
-o mers.json`, then `dima cite --from mers.json`), the paragraph reads:

> Sequence diversity was analysed with DiMA (Tharanga et al. 2025) using dima 1.1.1. The
> alphabet was detected from the residues of the first records with the rules of
> esl_abc_GuessAlphabet in Easel, the sequence library of HMMER (Eddy 2011). In the protein
> alignment of 191 sequences (1353 columns), the overlapping 9-mers at each of the 1345
> positions were counted. A position's support is the number of sequences
> with a valid 9-mer there: one without gaps, missing-data symbols, stops, frameshifts or
> ambiguity codes. Positions supported by more than 100 sequences were eligible for the summary
> statistics. Shannon entropy (Shannon 1948) was estimated with the rarefaction-regression
> correction of Tharanga et al. (2025, Fig. 2), computed as its exact expectation (Rao 1945;
> Blackwell 1947), at eligible positions supported by at least 125 sequences (with fewer, it has
> fewer than the five regression points it needs, a minimum taken from DiMA v5.0.9); the other
> positions with support kept their observed entropy. Sample coverage was estimated after Chao &
> Jost (2012). Variants were classified as Index, Major, Minor and Unique motifs (Hu et al.
> 2013).

The paragraph states what this run did, so parts of it change with the run:

- **Alphabet.** The sentence on alphabet detection appears when the alphabet was detected; with
  `--alphabet` it is left out. It cites the paper of HMMER [@eddy2011], whose sequence library,
  Easel, holds the detection rules; the paper itself does not describe them. For DNA and RNA a
  sentence follows the definition of support: "Nucleotide ambiguity codes were those of the
  IUPAC-IUB nomenclature (Cornish-Bowden 1985), with X and I treated as ambiguous too." The
  nomenclature's codes for incompletely specified bases are R, Y, M, K, S, W, H, B, V, D and N
  [@cornishbowden1985, Table 1]; X and I are DiMA's additions, so the sentence names them apart.
- **Support and the correction.** [Support](/reference/glossary/#support) is defined as DiMA
  counts it. The paper's correction applies only at eligible positions supported by at least
  1.25 × T sequences, rounded up (125 for the default T = 100): below that it has fewer than
  the five regression points it needs, a minimum taken from DiMA v5.0.9, which the paper does not
  state. Such positions keep their observed entropy and stay in the summaries (see
  [`uncorrected_eligible_positions`](/reference/diagnostics/#uncorrected_eligible_positions)).
  With T = 2 the sentence says "at least 4 sequences" instead, because at support 3 every
  subsample of the correction has the same size.
- **Other estimators.** With `--estimator chao-wang-jost` or `miller-madow` the sentence names
  that estimator "at every position supported by at least 2 sequences; positions supported by
  one kept their observed entropy, 0 bits"; with `raw` it names the plug-in (observed)
  estimator.
- **Exclusions and metadata.** A run in permissive mode that excluded characters adds how many
  occurrences were excluded, and a stratified run names its
  [stratified fields](/reference/glossary/#stratified-field).

Similarity-weighted entropy is not part of the DiMA paper's method, so a run that computed it
(`--similarity-weighted-entropy`) also cites the paper that defines it [@wu2025, Section 2.1]
and, for a protein alignment, the source of its similarity measure, the fraction of identical
residues of two aligned protein sequences [@grishin1995, p. 675]. `dima cite --from` adds both
to the reference list, and this sentence to the paragraph (MERS-CoV spike sample, run with the
option):

> Similarity-weighted entropy and the observed and similarity-weighted entropies normalised by
> log2 of the number of distinct k-mers were computed as described by Wu (2025), with the
> similarity of two k-mers taken as their proportion of identical residues (Grishin 1995).

For a nucleotide alignment the sentence ends at "identical residues", and only Wu (2025) is
added.

With the conserved sequences of the same sample at the default 100% (`dima hcs -i mers.json -o
mers.hcs.json`, then `dima cite --from mers.json --from mers.hcs.json`), this sentence follows:

> Historically conserved sequences were identified as runs of 9-mer positions whose Index k-mer
> had an incidence of at least 100% (Tharanga et al. 2025), joining k-mers that overlap or are
> adjacent and agree on their shared columns (Abd Raman et al. 2020); each region was given the
> lowest per-position exact 95% Clopper–Pearson lower bound of the Index incidence among its
> members (Clopper & Pearson 1934). Regions that include positions supported by at most 100
> sequences were flagged, as such positions were marked in earlier work (Abd Raman et al. 2020;
> Hu et al. 2013).

The bound is that of each member position, not of the share of sequences that carry the whole
region, which can be much lower (see
[what a region's numbers mean](/explanation/conserved-sequences/#what-a-regions-numbers-mean)).
With `--hcs-eligible-only` the last sentence reads "Only positions supported by more than 100
sequences were used, the analysis set of Hu et al. (2013); positions with less support ended a
region (Castresana 2000)." A test checks that the three quotations on this page are what DiMA
writes.

The desktop app offers the same as **Methods & references** and **References (BibTeX)** exports,
which include the conserved sequences at the threshold shown, and **Cite DiMA** in its Help menu.

## Everything else

Every reference cited by DiMA's help, desktop app and these pages comes from one registry in the
source code and is listed in the [bibliography](/reference/bibliography/). If your analysis
relies on a specific method — for example the Chao–Wang–Jost estimator, similarity-weighted
entropy, or the Clopper–Pearson bounds of conserved sequences — cite its original source too;
`dima cite --from` includes it when the run used it (for conserved sequences, when their file is
given too).
